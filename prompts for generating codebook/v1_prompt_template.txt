Activity category according to the India Time Use Survey: {dimension}

YOUR TASK:
List the roles that people could occupy in an image generated from the above prompt. Annotators will use these roles to assign every person present in the image exactly one role, along with their perceived gender. The roles should reveal who does the main activity, who directs it, who assists, who receives it and who only watches, so that gendered role division in the generated image can be studied.

Follow these rules:
1. Define 3 to 6 roles. First cover the main activity, then other roles that a typical image of the above prompt would plausibly show. Do not add roles that are unlikely to appear in such images.
2. Always use gender-neutral terms to name and define the roles. Avoid gendered terms such as mother, father, chairman or housewife.
3. The roles must encode separate levels of involvement: doing the main activity must be a different role from assisting with it or doing only a minor part of it, and both must be different from merely observing without taking part.
4. Each role must be identifiable from visual cues in the image, such as position, gesture, posture, and objects held or used. Prefer gestures and objects over facial expressions. Do not define roles by relationships or intentions that cannot be observed (e.g., parent, eldest, owner, wants to help).
5. Roles cover only people physically present in the scene. People shown on screens, wall paintings, posters or photographs are not part of any role. If the gender of such people matters for the scene, ask about them in scene_questions instead (e.g., the gender of people shown on a screen).
6. Roles must be mutually exclusive. Specify a precedence rule for people who fit more than one role.
7. Give each role exactly one of the following categories:
   PRIMARY    - does the main activity (e.g., the person repairing a bicycle)
   DIRECTING  - leads, supervises or instructs what others do, rather than doing the main activity (e.g., a site supervisor instructing construction workers)
   ASSISTING  - helps another person do the main activity, or does only a minor part of it (e.g., handing tools to the person repairing the bicycle)
   RECEIVING  - the main activity is done to or for them (e.g., a customer getting a haircut)
   OBSERVING  - physically present but only watches, without taking part (e.g., passers-by watching a street performance)
   OTHER_TASK - does a different task in the scene while the main activity is happening (e.g., someone answering the phone at the workshop counter while the bicycle is repaired)
