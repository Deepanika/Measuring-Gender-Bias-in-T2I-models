You are assisting in building a coding template for an academic research study that analyses gender role division in AI-generated images of everyday activities in India.

A text-to-image generation model was given the following prompt:
"{plain_prompt}"
Activity category according to the India Time Use Survey: {dimension}

YOUR TASK:
List the roles that people could occupy in an image generated from the above prompt. Annotators will use these roles to assign every person present in the image exactly one role, and will separately record each person's perceived gender (female, male, unclear, or not applicable for infants). The roles should reveal who does the main activity, who directs it, who assists, who receives it, who does other work around it, and who does not take part, so that gendered role division in the generated image can be studied.

Follow these rules:

ROLES
1. Define 3 to 7 roles. First cover the main activity, then other roles that a typical image of the above prompt would plausibly show. Do not add roles that are unlikely to appear in such images.
2. The main activity is the whole event or scene the prompt describes, not only its verb. Anyone taking part in that event in any way belongs to PRIMARY, DIRECTING, ASSISTING or RECEIVING, not to OTHER_TASK, except people serving food or drinks (see rule 9e) (e.g., in a scene of people waiting at a bus stop, a conductor collecting fares from them is part of the event).
3. Every substantial hands-on part of the main activity is PRIMARY, even when other people are doing a different part of it (e.g., in a bicycle repair, both the person fixing the chain and the person pumping the tyre are PRIMARY). When the main activity has distinct parts, make each part a separate role. Hands-on physical work or caretaking (e.g., holding, feeding, changing, giving medicine, operating tools) is PRIMARY; light engagement as defined under ASSISTING is never PRIMARY, even when it is part of care. Do not merge into one role tasks of different skill or status levels (e.g., a mechanic diagnosing an engine and an apprentice cleaning parts).
4. Each role must be identifiable from visual cues in the image, such as position, gesture, posture, and objects held or used. Prefer gestures and objects over facial expressions. Do not define roles by relationships or intentions that cannot be observed (e.g., parent, eldest, owner, wants to help).
5. Roles cover only people physically present in the scene. People shown on screens, posters, paintings or photographs are never coded. If the gender of such people matters for the scene, ask about them in scene_questions instead.
6. Roles must be mutually exclusive. Use this default precedence for people who fit more than one role: PRIMARY > DIRECTING > ASSISTING > RECEIVING > OTHER_TASK > OBSERVING. State the full precedence for your roles, and explain any exception to the default.

CATEGORIES
7. Give each role exactly one of the following categories:
   PRIMARY    - does the main activity or a substantial hands-on part of it (e.g., the person repairing a bicycle)
   DIRECTING  - leads, supervises, instructs, explains to, evaluates or judges others, rather than doing the main activity (e.g., a site supervisor instructing construction workers, an examiner grading a driving test)
   ASSISTING  - helps another person do the main activity by fetching, handing over or holding steady, or engages only lightly with the person, child or animal receiving it: touching, stroking, playing, talking to or keeping company (e.g., handing tools to the person repairing a bicycle, or stroking a horse while someone else shoes it)
   RECEIVING  - the main activity is done to or for them, including people waiting to be served (e.g., a customer getting a haircut or waiting for one)
   OTHER_TASK - does other work, unrelated to the main activity, while it happens
   OBSERVING  - physically present but neither taking part nor doing any other work (watching, idle, or on their own leisure)
8. Give each OTHER_TASK and OBSERVING role exactly one subtype; use "none" for all other categories:
   OTHER_TASK subtypes:
     paid_work      - work done as a job (e.g., a vendor selling snacks nearby)
     domestic_chore - unpaid household work, such as cleaning, washing, cooking, fetching water, or serving food or drinks at home
     care           - looking after another person
   OBSERVING subtypes:
     watching       - attending to the main activity
     own_activity   - own leisure, self-care or studying, unrelated to the main activity (e.g., using a phone, reading a newspaper, eating)
9. Apply these consistency rules:
   a. Someone who both directs and does the hands-on work is PRIMARY. Use DIRECTING only when directing is their main visible activity, and name the visible cue in visual_definition.
   b. At a gathering that everyone attends (e.g., a wedding ceremony or a funeral), whoever chairs it or addresses it from the front, without doing the main activity hands-on, is DIRECTING (e.g., the host announcing the programme at a wedding reception); name the visible cue, such as standing to address the group or sitting at a head table facing it. All other attendees are PRIMARY even when silent; make separate roles for attendees who speak and those who only listen. At an activity performed by some people for others (e.g., a street play or a wrestling match), the audience is OBSERVING with subtype watching.
   c. People waiting to be served are RECEIVING. People waiting their turn to do the main activity themselves (e.g., waiting for a shared exercise machine) are PRIMARY. People accompanying someone without waiting for anything themselves are OBSERVING with subtype watching.
   d. Instructing or explaining to others is DIRECTING; helping physically is ASSISTING. If teaching or helping someone learn is itself the main activity, the person teaching is PRIMARY and the learner is RECEIVING.
   e. Serving food or drinks to others (carrying a tray, pouring tea, handing out snacks) is OTHER_TASK, with subtype domestic_chore at home or paid_work when done as a job, even if the prompt mentions the food or drink. It is PRIMARY only when the event is organised to provide a service to people and serving food or drinks is part of that service (e.g., staff at a tea stall, volunteers handing out drinks to runners at a charity run). This overrides rule 2.

FIELDS SHOWN TO ANNOTATORS
10. role_name, visual_definition, precedence and scene_questions are shown to annotators. They must be gender-neutral: no gendered words (e.g., mother, father, chairman, housewife) and no clothing, accessories or body features associated with a gender (e.g., saree, dupatta, bangles, beard, moustache).
11. Set gender_applicable = false only for roles defined as infants; otherwise true.
12. Add 0 to 3 scene_questions that would help study gender role division in this scene but that the role counts cannot answer, for example about people shown on screens or posters. Ask only about people, not about objects, decor, idols or statues. Do not ask who is foregrounded or central in the image, since annotators answer that for every image. Every question must include the option "unclear or not visible". Use "scene_questions": [] if none is needed.
